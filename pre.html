Introduction & SMART Goals

Purpose: To investigate the correlation between frequent AI usage and the depth of independent reasoning among IT students[cite: 298].

Research Questions:

  • How does regular AI tool interaction affect the development of critical thinking? [cite: 301]
  • To what extent do students verify AI-generated content? [cite: 303]
  • Does AI usage lead to significant "cognitive offloading"? [cite: 599]

"Chatting robots (chatbots) simulate intelligent human language interaction" (Pokrivcakova, 2019)[cite: 581]. This research tests if this simulation replaces student's own cognitive processes.

Methodology

Mixed-Method Approach[cite: 307]:

1. Quantitative: Online survey via Microsoft Forms (n=68).

2. Qualitative: In-depth interview with a female undergraduate student to identify underlying patterns of behavior[cite: 322].

3. Analysis: Thematic Analysis (Braun & Clarke) used for transcript coding[cite: 499].

Survey Results (Data Insights)

Demographics: Year of Study

70.6% of respondents are 2nd-year students[cite: 550].

Frequency of AI Usage

76.5% use AI tools "Always" or "Often"[cite: 550].

Main Tasks for AI

Summarizing and assignment completion are primary drivers[cite: 550].

Verification Frequency

A significant gap exists between usage and cross-checking[cite: 550].

Interview Thematic Analysis

Key Themes Identified:

  • Theme 1: Cognitive Offloading. "Writing ideas simply first, then refining them using AI"[cite: 515].
  • Theme 2: Trust vs. Skepticism. "I realized I depended too much on the AI instead of creating my own ideas"[cite: 555].
  • Theme 3: Skill Deterioration. "Reduced effort in self-correction"[cite: 517].

Interview Saturation:

Both survey and interview data suggest AI acts as a "support tool" but risks becoming a "full replacement" for thinking if not balanced[cite: 542].

Discussion & Literature Comparison

Findings:

Our data indicates that while AI improves speed (79.4% [cite: 404]), it creates a **Verification Gap**. Students trust the logic of AI if it "makes sense" based on prior knowledge, rather than using peer-reviewed databases[cite: 555, 560].

Theoretical Framework (Scroll):

Cahn (2021): Defines chatbots as dialogue systems between humans and machines[cite: 583]. Our study finds this dialogue is increasingly one-sided.

Pokrivcakova (2019): Learners can learn through direct communication with a robot[cite: 586]. However, our "Theme 1" shows that many students use it for output, not learning.

Yavich (2025): Emphasizes the mediating role of cognitive offloading[cite: 599]. Our survey confirms a "Moderate reduction" in independent reasoning for most participants[cite: 550].

Conclusion

AI tools are beneficial for productivity but pose a threat to critical thinking if used as a primary source of reasoning[cite: 541]. Future academic success depends on balancing AI support with rigorous independent analysis[cite: 543].
